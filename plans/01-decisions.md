| 54 | Backup power is a modeled microgrid, not a flag | **2026-08-02.** `Circuit.backup: bool` is replaced by `Circuit.backup_tier: BackupTier \| None` (`ALWAYS_ON` / `SHED`) plus `Circuit.source: bool` and an authored `Circuit.duty_cycle`. | 55 | Humidity is its own axis | **2026-08-18.** A room that is *run* wet gets `Room.humidity_class` (`NORMAL` / `WET` / `HUMID`) plus optional `design_relative_humidity` / `design_temperature_f`, not a new `Occupancy` member. Occupancy describes *use* — the plant room genuinely is `LIVING` and the sauna genuinely is `BATHROOM` — and a separate axis is the only shape that lets showers, sauna and plant room share one set of rules. `WET` is intermittent (dries between sessions), `HUMID` continuous (no drying window, so no forgiveness). The axis is what scopes `building_science.condensation` **per (wall, interior room)** instead of per assembly tag: the same stack is a different question at 70 % RH than at 35 %, and before this the walk analysed every assembly at the whole-house figure and read `assembly.default_lining`, so a 70 %-RH room would have been checked at 35 %. Four rules key on it — `building_science.humid_room_liner` (a Class I `{VAPOR, AIR}` layer on the room side of every bounding assembly's core), `humid_room_finish` (no paper-faced gypsum showing), `glazing_dew_point` (inner glass surface vs the room's own dew point, house-wide and genuinely new), and `mep.humid_room_pressure` (a continuously humid room with supply and no matched extract) (→ houses/catlin/notes/plant_room.md). |
| 56 | The humid side is a wall *type* | **2026-08-18.** RM-S-PLANT's liner follows the sauna precedent exactly — `PLANT_EXT_2X6_HUMID` / `PLANT_INT_2X6_BRG_HUMID` / `PLANT_INT_2X4_HUMID`, not a `Room.wall_lining` override — for three reasons that each decide it alone: the liner changes the wall's thickness (a lining override may not), an asymmetric wall needs `interior_room` to name the face it lands on, and only a type is a thing the condensation walk and #55's rules can see. **The panel is not the vapour barrier.** No PVC or FRP maker publishes a perm rating, so the control layer is a separate sealed membrane behind the panel, and the model says so the only honest way: `pvc-panel` and `vinyl-sheet` carry no permeance at all (per #46's convention) and the membrane carries a *specification* value with its basis in `source=`. The consequence is deliberate — `building_science.condensation` reports UNKNOWN on those assemblies, naming the panel, and `humid_room_liner` is the rule that answers. Interior walls align on the stud centreline (`face("stud-ext", offset=…)`, the W-B-CS idiom) so the liner grows into the humid room and the bearing grid does not move (#43). **Later the same day the sauna's own fourth face followed the same rule**: `W-B-S2`'s south side was bare concrete because every non-structural layer of `CATLIN_BASEMENT_12_GARDEN` sits *outboard* of the pour, so it took `SAUNA_LINER_ON_BASEMENT_12_GARDEN` — a wall type, not a lining override, for the same three reasons. A concrete-backed variant needs **no** alignment offset: it stays on `face("concrete-ext")` because on that wall the concrete's outboard face *is* the datum, so the pour does not move and only the liner grows inward. W-B-CS's `offset=inch(-6)` exists solely to re-centre its concrete on the 18' bearing grid, which is a different problem. And a liner on a foundation wall states its own ceiling: the three liner layers carry a `LayerExtent` off `WALL_TOP` so a 7'-6" room does not bill 9'-0" of basswood. |
| 57 | A rim cavity is a `ConstructionRule`, not a `Transition` | **2026-08-18.** RM-S-PLANT's rim joists (§56) needed closed-cell foam to keep the Class I barrier continuous past `FS-SECOND`/`FS-ATTIC`, and a `Transition` (#37) cannot bill it — a `Transition` documents a detail sheet, it cannot mutate the takeoff. | 58 | A split layer row is one depth slot | **2026-08-20.** `Layer.extent` (#—) bands a layer vertically, and its docstring always promised the further case: two layers with non-overlapping extents as the two regions of *one row* of the stack — a parge below grade under a panel above it, a brick plinth under a different brick field. The resolver never implemented it, so each region still advanced the stack depth and a four-colour brick wythe resolved four wythes deep. **`Layer.slot` names the row**: layers sharing one occupy a single depth position and borrow one strip, exactly as cavity insulation already does inside its host. Rules, enforced by `integrity.assembly_layers`: one thickness, every region banded, no two overlapping — a *gap* stays legal, because that is what a reveal is. Consumers that measure the stack read `depth_layers()` (R-value, wall thickness); consumers that bill area or draw a band read every region (`wall_structure` bills each colour on its own row, and `prices.toml` may key a rate by material). Catlin's Ishtar veneer is the first use. |
| 59 | A storey's deck may be more than one thing | **2026-08-21.** The main floor over catlin's basement is two `FloorSystem`s and one `Slab`, not one element: 819 SF of 11 7/8" I-joists at 16" o.c. and a 414 SF band of 8" EPS stay-in-place form with a 4 5/8" cast cap, over the dining end where the radiant zone wants thermal mass. It replaced 1,233 SF x 9" of cast suspended concrete — the largest line in the model, 34.26 cy, whose low price was commercially unobtainable because a shoring sub will not mobilise for less than $25-40k, and which forced eight interior 12" concrete cross walls with strip footings under them purely so it had something to span between. **The two systems are interchangeable because their depths are equal**: 8" + 4 5/8" = 12 5/8" = 11 7/8" joist + 3/4" subfloor, so both meet at one soffit plane and one finished-floor plane and both span 18'-0" to the x=18' bearing line. Re-apportioning the ceiling later is moving one boundary line, which is the property the equal depth was chosen for, not a side effect. Consequences that are decisions in their own right: `FloorSystem.outline` scopes only the perpendicular extent, so a storey split between two joist fields needs one system per span (one naming all three bearing lines frames straight through the concrete); the depth constants live in `params/main_deck.py` rather than in the editable storey file, because an editable-dialect file may hold only literals and the whole point is that the arithmetic is one line; and `integrity.slab_thickness` now asserts a slab's authored thickness lands on one of its assembly's layer boundaries, so a cap edited without the depth cannot pass quietly. |

| 62 | A run is one solid, and its fittings are counted | **2026-08-24.** `ResolvedSolid` was a plan ring extruded straight up in Z, which is the only shape a prism can speak — so everything that rakes or slopes was *faked* by chopping it into level pieces. `RL-A-HANDRAIL`, one straight 13-ft bar, was **292 solids** (one band per 1-1/2" of fall, times `rail_count`, times four round-facet bands) and railings were 1,149 of catlin's 2,857; a sloping drain was stair-stepped into at most three level stacks by a function whose own docstring called that "an accepted approximation". Each piece was a separate glTF node, a separate `IfcRailing`, and a separate polyline on the plan sheet. **`SolidSweep` — a closed section carried along a 3D polyline — is the fix, and `resolve/sweep.py` is the one kernel both trades and the viewer mirror** (`ui/src/three/tubeGeometry.ts`, pinned vertex-for-vertex by `ui/src/generated/sweepParity.json`). A run mitres at its interior vertices and butts past 80° of turn, which in the field *is* an elbow fitting; each leg is one `GBox`, which glTF, IFC and the section slice kernel already spoke, so sections through a rail or a sloped pipe became correct for free. Catlin went 2,857 solids → 953. **No new solid `category`**, so the trade/palette parity gates are untouched, and `outline`/`z0_m`/`z1_m` still carry the whole run's plan silhouette and Z extents so an untaught consumer degrades honestly rather than breaking. Three consequences are decisions in their own right. **The grade is now the authored fact**: an `elevations` entry may be `None` and `PipeRun.slope_in_per_ft` solves it over developed plan length, because a drain that falls uniformly for forty feet is one fact and twelve hand-computed inverts off it are twelve chances to be wrong — a vertical leg is the one thing that cannot solve (no plan run to fall over) and errors. **Fittings are counted, not estimated**: elbows off each run's true 3D polyline snapped to a stock 90/45/22.5 within the 10° a bend absorbs on a pitched branch, wyes off `drain_tie_ins`'s real parent inference — which replaces a plan-only 20° test with a hard-coded `return 90.0` in it, and an O(n²) shared-vertex guess. **So the pipe pricing basis changed**: `[pipe_runs]`'s `drain`/`vent` `$/LF` said in its own note that it included "a share of fittings", which was the only place a fitting could ride when they were an estimate; those two rows are re-based to BARE PIPE and the adder moved into a new `[pipe_fittings]` table by the piece. Restoring it would double-bill every elbow in the house, so both notes say so and a test pins the rate. Supply *tees* are still not counted — there is no parent inference for a pressurised system — so the supply rows keep their adder and their old numbers, deliberately. **Two consumers had to be taught that a `GBox` is no longer always upright.** The section kernel's fast path read a box's bottom ring as a *plan footprint* paired with the top ring above it — true of every member, panel and closure band, and false of a tube leg, whose rings are separated along the leg axis — so a horizontal pipe's footprint degenerated to a line and every drain silently vanished from every section cut. A swept leg is convex (a convex profile swept straight, capped by two planes), so it is cut by taking the hull of its edge crossings: no welding, nothing to fall open where a 12-gon's own vertex row lands on the cut, and 8,766 cuts agree with the general mesh walk to 2e-5 of area. And IFC exported every run **twice** — as the real `IfcPipeSegment`s/`IfcCableCarrierSegment`s the dedicated emitters write, *and* as the generic solid, which has no `_SOLID_IFC_CLASS` row and so landed on the `IfcFooting` fallback: 68 of catlin's 239 footings were pipe. The generic loop now skips `ROUTED_RUN_CATEGORIES` for exactly the reason it already skipped the pipe accessories. |

## Naming & rename-ease strategy

Name is now locked (#9: `Type:Haus` / PyPI `typehaus` / domain `type-haus.com` / CLI `haus`),
but the codebase stays engineered for a cheap rename if one is ever needed:

- **One import root:** all Python code lives under `packages/engine/src/typehaus/`. No
  sub-package embeds the brand (they're `typehaus.model`, `typehaus.emit`, …). A rename =
  rename one directory + one `pyproject.toml` `name` + find/replace `typehaus` imports.
- **CLI is `haus`**, not `typehaus` — user muscle memory survives a rename.
- **Brand strings centralized:** `typehaus/_meta.py` exposes `PROJECT_NAME`, `PROJECT_URL`,
  `IFC_APP_NAME` (used in IFC headers, pset prefixes `Pset_TH_*` — keep the pset prefix short
  and brand-agnostic: use `Pset_TH_Source` but define the prefix as a constant in `_meta.py`).
- **UI:** brand only in one `branding.ts` constant file.
- Include a `docs/RENAME.md` checklist (pyproject name, src dir, npm package name, GitHub
  repo, docs site, `_meta.py`, `branding.ts`).

## Reviewer items considered and rejected/deferred (so they don't resurface unexamined)

- **Loro / CRDT sync** — rejected again (decision #4's rationale is unchanged): the round-trip
  counterparty is an architect's tool rewriting files wholesale, invisible to any CRDT; local
  concurrency is handled by #30's preconditions + locking; multi-user collaboration is git's
  job. Revisit only if real-time co-editing becomes a goal, which it is not (→ 00 §Non-goals).
- **"Reframe round-trip as controlled change review"** — already the design: (→ 20 §Diff) *is*
  a change review (semantic diff → per-change accept/reject via `/import-review` → decision log
  in the handoff bundle). No change needed.
- **`library.lock`** — deferred, not rejected: while `library/` ships inside the engine
  package, the `requires_engine` pin (#31) transitively pins every library item. A lockfile
  becomes necessary exactly when library items are distributed/versioned independently of
  engine releases — add it then, not before.
- **Built-in regional cost data** — rejected (#28): guaranteed wrong somewhere, high
  maintenance, and it would launder guesses into authority. User-supplied `prices.toml` gives
  the same UI with honest provenance.
- **Persistent constraint solver for dimensions** — rejected in favor of the anchor-pin scheme
  (#26): no solver in the < 2 s edit loop, no over/under-constrained failure modes, every
  dimension edit stays one journaled op. Revisit only if real usage shows anchor pins are
  insufficient.
- **Guardrail / handrail code enforcement** — deferred as a *check*, not built as a modeled
  system. Guard height, baluster spacing, and handrail graspability (R312 / R311.7.8) are
  finish-stage decisions easily resolved on site; a design-time code gate here would mostly
  generate noise and frustrate users chasing a permit-plan bar that finishing will settle
  anyway. Guards/rails can be drawn as ordinary `Annotation` symbols where a drawing calls for
  them; the engine does not model them as elements or check them. Revisit only if a
  jurisdiction demands them on the submitted plan.
- **A dedicated through-wall ERV for the plant room** — rejected on the manufacturer's own
  numbers (2026-08-18, houses/catlin/notes/plant_room.md). The unit on the table is out of
  spec below −4 °F against a −15 °F design temperature, states a design limit of "RH below
  80 %" with the target at 70 %, moves 6–15 CFM in actual recovery mode, publishes no latent
  performance, and — decisively — is *unbalanced*: a single core alternates supply and
  exhaust on 75-second half-cycles, so it cyclically pressurises the room, which is precisely
  what destroys a humid room's envelope. A dedicated, dampered, RH-controlled branch off the
  house's existing `EQ-B-ERV` gives the isolation that was actually wanted (an independent
  *damper*, not an independent *machine*) using a mechanical room that already has a
  condensate drain and real frost control. Revisit only with a unit that publishes latent
  recovery and runs balanced at the design temperature.
- **Early Glaser condensation check** — considered during the plan review (it needs only
  layers + materials + design temps, so it *could* run from M1); decided to keep it in M5 per
  #42. The assembly section card (→ 12 §Assembly card) moves early instead; the Glaser plot
  lands on that card when M5 arrives.

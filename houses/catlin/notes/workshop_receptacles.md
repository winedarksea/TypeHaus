# Workshop receptacles

The two west-wall bench receptacles stay at their existing stations and 42 inches above the
finished floor. Both are surface-mounted to concrete, above the 34-inch benches.

RC1 is a 20 A combination 5-20R/6-20R receptacle, with the
[Leviton 5842](https://leviton.com/products/5842) as its configuration basis. Its new
`CKT-WORKSHOP-240` is on panel 2, positions 17 and 19, with a common-trip two-pole GFCI
breaker. Bring both hot legs, neutral and equipment ground in a protected surface raceway;
connect the device using the manufacturer's common-feed diagram. Both voltages share the
breaker capacity; this is not two independent 20 A supplies. The circuit's 4,800 VA is a
conservative full-breaker planning allowance, not a known electronics nameplate. Continuous
loads need the appropriate 125% sizing. The route and actual connected loads remain to be
verified; the model does not author a conductor-by-conductor branch route.

RC2 is a four-outlet 20 A 5-20R, based on the
[Leviton 21254-W](https://leviton.com/products/21254-w), still on the GFCI/AFCI-protected
`CKT-RC-BSMT`. Four sockets do not create additional circuit capacity. Leviton's
[instructions](https://leviton.com/content/dam/leviton/commercial-industrial/product_documents/instruction_sheet/PK-A3158-10-02-0F-W.pdf)
include a 4-inch square box with a single-gang plaster ring. Use that arrangement for RC2,
surface mounted, with a ring and cover compatible with the device. For RC1 use a 4-inch
square surface box and a compatible single-device raised cover. Size each box for the actual
conductors, clamps, grounds and device yoke; bond metal boxes and raceways. Confirm box depth,
fill and cover compatibility for both locations with the final listed products.
RC1 retains the shared combination type's schematic 4-inch depth; replace that envelope
with the selected box and cover's measured projection when the hardware is specified.

**Product selection remains open.** Neither linked product page establishes that its device
is tamper-resistant. Accessible 15/20 A, 125/250 V nonlocking receptacles in a dwelling
generally require listed tamper-resistant devices under
[NEC 2023 406.12](https://captaincode2023.leviton.com/node/340); confirm the locally adopted
code and any applicable exception. Obtain compliant listed devices with these configurations,
or revise the installation to separately listed tamper-resistant 120 V and 240 V receptacles
and equivalent four 120 V positions. A cover or outlet plugs alone are not an established
substitute for a listed tamper-resistant receptacle. Confirm the electrician's final GFCI,
AFCI, raceway, bonding and conductor design before construction.

The existing kitchen combination receptacle uses the same catalog type but a separate
`CKT-KETTLE`. Its 120 V half also requires a neutral and a compatible protected feed; the
current single circuit reference does not by itself establish the installed conductors.

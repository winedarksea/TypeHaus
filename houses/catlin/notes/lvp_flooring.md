# Quincy Oak LVP and concrete cap coordination

Provisional owner selection, 2026-10-26: **COREtec Pro Classics Quincy Oak,
VV017-01018**, click-lock floating SPC. The [manufacturer product page](https://coretecfloors.com/en-us/products/coretec-pro-classics/quincy-oak-vv017-01018)
lists nominal **7 × 48-inch planks, 5 mm total thickness including attached cork,
20 mil wear layer, UV acrylic finish and microbeveled edges**, 28.84 SF/carton.
The listed $5.69/SF material price is an allowance pending a local delivered quote;
existing floating-floor installation labor remains separate. Confirm carton quantity,
waste/cut layout, freight, tax, current stock and the physical sample before ordering.

## Appearance and installation

The model uses the mean color of the central half of COREtec's
[vv017_01018_1to1 plank image](https://shawfloors.widen.net/content/2stmgfpfwp/jpeg/vv017_01018_1to1?crop=false&fmt=jpeg&h=0&quality=85&w=840),
**#bfb6ad**. This is a pale warm gray oak photographic approximation, not a measured
reflectance or a guarantee of the delivered color. The existing low-sheen procedural
7 × 48-inch plank recipe supplies oak grain and shallow microbevels. Its grain and
six-print repeat are schematic, not the manufacturer's actual print set. Check a
sample in the house light; the goal remains the lightest white wood appearance.

Use the attached cork only: no separate acoustic mat, no extra mat charge and no
assumption that a pad compresses to zero height. COREtec's
[installation guide](https://pdmsview.shawinc.com/USFloors/COREtec-Pro/Installation/Residential-Installation-Guidelines-for-SPC-Produc)
requires a clean, dry, structurally sound subfloor, flat within 3/16 inch in 10 feet
or 1/8 inch in 6 feet, and 1/4-inch perimeter expansion for areas below 2,500 SF.
Do not lay sheet plastic over the wood subfloor. Its
[second-underlayment bulletin](https://pdmsview.shawinc.com/Technical-Bulletins/Hard-Surface/Additional-cork-under-pro-plus-enhanced)
allows residential secondary underlayment up to 3 mm when fully adhered, but reports
no significant additional acoustic benefit; that option is not selected here.
Keep floating edges free under trims and fixed cabinetry independently supported.

## Clean cap dimensions versus flooring thickness

Keep the existing flat bearing seat at −13 7/16 inches, 10-inch EPS form,
3/4-inch framed plywood and **4 3/8-inch concrete cap**. Heights are above the
main top-of-joist datum. Concrete top remains **+0.9375 inch**; Quincy Oak top is
**+0.9468504 inch**, a nominal **0.0093504-inch** difference.

With these fixed structural depths, exact nominal equality would require:

**Cap thickness = 4 3/16 inches + installed flooring thickness in inches.**

| Total LVP depth including attached pad | Nearby cap dimension | LVP relative to concrete |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 4 mm | 4 3/8 in | 0.0300 in lower |
| **5 mm — Quincy Oak / Whitmore White** | **4 3/8 in** | **0.0094 in higher** |
| 6 mm | 4 7/16 in | 0.0138 in lower |
| 7 mm — Cotton Oak rigid core | 4 7/16 in | 0.0256 in higher |
| 8 mm | 4 1/2 in | 0.0025 in higher |

These are calculated nominal comparisons, excluding coating thickness, not approvals
for alternate structural caps. All are within the project's nominal 1/16-inch flush
target. A cap change requires coordinating the assembly layers, concrete quantity,
reinforcement/cover, published span basis and structural review. The 4 1/2-inch option
also exceeds the current bearing-seat check's 1/4-inch cap-to-bare-plywood allowance;
a future finished-surface-aware check is needed before selecting it.

The nominal 3/4-inch plywood is another coordination assumption: measure the actual
panel and installed plank and the selected concrete coating. Set the finished edge
flush from samples and survey levels; do not specify an impractical thousandth-inch
pour tolerance or use an unspecified compressible mat to tune it. A deliberately specified,
manufacturer-accepted secondary underlay can form part of the stack, as discussed below. Preserve both legs of the
L-shaped material boundary and the y=13-foot movement break described in
[mixed-deck movement joint](mixed_deck_movement_joint.md). Flush finish planes still
need a floating-floor-compatible edge/movement detail.

## Pour convenience and the full installed stack

Owner comparison, 2026-10-26. **14 3/8 inches is the total depth from the common
bearing seat to the concrete top, not 14 3/8 inches of solid concrete.** It comprises
10 inches of EPS form/rib depth and a 4 3/8-inch cap. A 14 1/2-inch total depth means
a 4 1/2-inch cap on that same form and seat. The concrete below the cap also fills
the form's beam channels.

A half-inch dimension is easier to communicate, but there is no demonstrated
placement advantage here sufficient to choose the flooring around it. Screeding is
to a specified elevation: [Somero's guidance](https://www.somero.com/resources/learning-center/)
describes checking grade using a transmitter, receiver and grade stick. Our inference
is that setting this surface 1/8 inch higher is a grade-setting change, not a simpler
concrete placement method. Ask the actual form/finishing crew whether its edge forms
or screed guides make either choice cheaper. The finish elevation, adequate concrete
cover, flatness and the movement detail matter more than the fraction's appearance.

The full flooring stack includes the attached pad exactly once, plus the installed
thickness of any approved secondary underlayment and adhesive/leveling layers.
For this house, excluding concrete coating thickness:

**Matching total deck depth = 14 3/16 inches + full flooring stack in inches.**

| Total deck depth from seat | Cap above 10-inch form | Ideal full flooring stack | Nearby practical stack and nominal mismatch |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 14 3/8 in | 4 3/8 in | 4.7625 mm | 5 mm: floor 0.2375 mm (0.0094 in) high |
| **14 1/2 in** | **4 1/2 in** | **7.9375 mm** | **8 mm: floor 0.0625 mm (0.0025 in) high** |
| 14 9/16 in | 4 9/16 in | 9.5250 mm | 10 mm: floor 0.4750 mm (0.0187 in) high |
| 14 5/8 in | 4 5/8 in | 11.1125 mm | 10 mm: floor 1.1125 mm (0.0438 in) low; 11 mm: 0.1125 mm (0.0044 in) low |

At a 14 1/2-inch total deck, 10 mm flooring sits **2.0625 mm (0.0812 inch) high**,
which exceeds our nominal 1/16-inch flush target. Additional underlay only raises it
further. A 14 5/8-inch total deck is the nearby eighth-inch option for that product;
14 9/16 inches gives a closer nominal fit without extra layers. These remain
comparisons, not selected structural changes. Concrete coating and actual plywood
thickness alter the target stack and must be included in the field setout.

### Can secondary underlayment tune the height?

Yes, where the exact product and installation method allow it. The selected 5 mm
Quincy Oak plus a specified 3 mm secondary underlayment gives an approximately 8 mm
stack and pairs with a 14 1/2-inch total deck. This is a viable coordination option,
not the current selection and not an assumption that any 3 mm foam is acceptable.
The [Shaw/COREtec bulletin](https://pdmsview.shawinc.com/Technical-Bulletins/Hard-Surface/Additional-cork-under-pro-plus-enhanced)
permits a secondary layer up to 3 mm under its covered residential attached-pad
vinyl products and requires full adhesion to the subfloor. Its testing does not
establish significant extra acoustic benefit. Select a compatible, adequately firm
underlay and adhesive; count the installed bondline and obtain product-specific
acceptance. Do not substitute a nominal 1/8-inch (3.175 mm) mat for the 3 mm maximum,
stack multiple soft mats, or count on compressing one to an exact elevation.

For Mohawk, the [PureTech installation instructions](https://s7d4.scene7.com/is/content/MohawkResidential/mhk_puretech_installation_instructions)
allow optional additional underlay for attached-pad floors over wood and require
following the underlay instructions; the [Native Ridge sell sheet](https://s7d4.scene7.com/is/content/MohawkResidential/mhk_puretech_plus_nativeridge_prod_sellsheet)
lists Performance Accessories options. This is not blanket approval for an arbitrary
1 mm pad to make a desired stack. The Talc Oak attached 2 mm pad already counts in
its 10 mm product thickness.

**Recommendation:** keep the current 5 mm / 14 3/8-inch pairing if Quincy Oak wins
on appearance and price. If deliberately selecting a 14 1/2-inch total deck, target
an approximately 8 mm full stack, preferably a suitable product with its attached
pad alone; an approved Quincy Oak + 3 mm layer is the alternative. Consider Mohawk
at its actual 10 mm total, with a correspondingly higher cap, if it wins the product
comparison. Do not change the structural cap just to avoid the fraction 3/8.

# Opening flashing — performance spec, qualifying products, application

Researched 2026-09-19 from the evaluation reports, TDSs, application guides and the one
published ponding study that argues against this class of product.

Covers the membrane at every window rough opening: the sill pan, the jamb band and the head
band on the plywood buck, and the lap onto the shaved ccSPF. It is the `[K5]` scope of
`notes/outie_window_truss_detail.md` and nothing else — `[K6]`'s metal head flashing and
`[K7]`'s sealant at the cladding-to-frame joint are separate parts bought elsewhere.

**The spec is the requirement, not the brand.** Any product that meets §1 may be bought.

**Why this house takes a fluid-applied membrane and not tape.** There is no sheet WRB here:
4" of exterior ccSPF is the air/water/vapour/thermal plane (`notes/ccspf_spec.md`), so at an
opening there is nothing for a tape to lap *onto* — the water-plane tie is plywood buck to
spray foam. And the sill is a shape both tape and formed metal do badly: back dam up the
buck's inboard leg, out 6" to the mount plane, then **down over the buck's front edge with
no visible drip** (`[K5]` rejects a visible drip explicitly). That is two inside corners and a turn-down,
forty times over. A liquid makes it in one pass and removes the origami at the corners, which
is where tape actually fails.

It also backs the foam's WRB listing. The selected foam, Enverge OnePass HFO, lists WRB in
its own report (IAPMO ER-859 §3.5; `notes/ccspf_spec.md`); a substitute without that listing
makes the water plane a Minn. R. 1300.0110 request. Openings are where a reviewer's water question lives,
and a membrane with its own ICC-ES number at every one of them is the strongest answer the
detail can give.

## 1. Requirements

Required:

1. **A CURRENT evaluation report under AC148** (fluid-applied flashing at rough openings).
   A lapsed report does not qualify, whatever the product was. Note what such a report
   evaluates and what it does not: AC148 grades exactly one property, **water resistance**,
   and nothing about vapour, air, adhesion in service or durability.
2. **The buck is the substrate, and it must be on the report's list.** These are 3/8"
   **plywood** bucks — fully in scope of every qualifying report below.
3. **Written substrate approval for the lap onto shaved ccSPF.** ⚠ **OPEN — this needs a
   phone call and a letter, not an estimate.** ESR-4363's substrate whitelist is OSB,
   plywood, gypsum, MgO, wood, galvanized and stainless steel, vinyl/PVC, concrete, masonry
   and anodized aluminium. **Spray polyurethane foam is not on it**, and AC148 does not
   evaluate it. The ICC-ES number is therefore NOT the authority for the one lap that makes
   the water plane continuous here. What closes it is (a) a written substrate approval from
   the membrane maker naming shaved 2 lb ccSPF, and (b) a **jobsite adhesion mock-up** on
   the actual foam, shaved to the actual gauge, pulled before the first opening is flashed.
   Do not let the report number stand in for either.
4. **Build the sill heavy.** Tremco publishes **60 mils at sills** against 20 mils
   elsewhere; PROSOCO specifies 12-15 mils everywhere with no sill increase. Take the
   heavier reading regardless of which product is bought: the sill is the contested case
   (§1.1) and the extra material is the cheapest insurance in the detail.
5. **A wet-film gauge on site**, used. A fluid-applied membrane's whole performance claim is
   a thickness claim, and "it looked thick enough" is not one.

Strongly preferred, in this order:

6. Report covering **2018 IRC**, which is this profile's base (`checks/code/mn_residential`).
7. AAMA 714 (liquid-applied flashing) in addition to AC148.
8. A published **cure-temperature** and **recoat window** in the TDS, not only in a FAQ.

### 1.1 The ponding finding, and why it does not govern this pan

**A reader will find this paper, so the note says it first.** RDH's 2017 study
(Wilkinson / Tatara / Finch) ponded seven liquid membranes on wood sills for 42 days. The
liquids reached **25-40% MC** against a <20% threshold. Holladay's independent test agreed:
*"None remained waterproof under a puddle."* There is no rebuttal and no follow-up testing
(2023-2026).

Two things about that result decide whether it reaches this detail:

- **The permeable self-adhered CONTROL failed identically** (~35-40% MC). Only the
  *impermeable* self-adhered tape passed (~10-15%). The paper's own verbatim conclusion is
  *"No discernable drying benefit to liquid applied membrane flashings over impermeable
  self-adhered products."* It is not a finding that liquids are worse than tapes; it is a
  finding about permeable membranes under standing water.
- **Every one of those failures was a PONDING failure, and this pan does not pond.** The
  `[K5]` pan falls 1/4"/ft over 6" to the mount plane and turns **down over the buck's front
  edge**, so water that reaches it leaves it. A pan that drains has no puddle to sit under for 42 days. That
  is the whole reason the finding does not govern here.

**Where it does still bite:** during construction, before the cladding and its vent gap
exist, and on any opening left open to weather. Flash, then close in. A pan standing full of
February meltwater for three weeks is the paper's test, run on your building.

**The permitted alternative.** An **impermeable self-adhered** pan at the sill with the
liquid at the corners, jambs and head is the hybrid the ponding data actually favours, and
it is ~$30/opening in material against ~$112 for the full fluid system. It is acceptable
here on the same reasoning and against the same §1 requirements. What is NOT acceptable is a
*permeable* self-adhered pan, which the paper puts level with the liquids it criticises.

| Product | Report | Substrates incl. plywood | Sill thickness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PROSOCO **FastFlash** (R-Guard) | ICC-ES **ESR-4363**, AC148, 2018 IRC | yes | 12-15 mils, no sill increase published | The class reference. Applies below 32 F on frost-free substrate but **will not cure** until above freezing — see §3 |
| Tremco **ExoAir LF / liquid flashing** | AC148 | yes | **60 mils at sills**, 20 elsewhere | The heavier sill spec; take this reading on any product |
| Self-adhered **impermeable** pan (hybrid) | per maker, AAMA 711 | yes | sheet | The §1.1 alternative at the sill only; liquid still at corners, jambs and head |

Not qualifying:

- **Any permeable self-adhered flashing** at the sill. §1.1 — it failed the ponding test
  identically to the liquids and buys nothing the liquid does not.
- Any product whose report does not name **plywood**. The buck is the substrate that matters.
- A sealant or a caulk. This is a membrane; a bead in a corner is not one.

## 3. Application

**Foam first, flash after, and the order is not negotiable.** `notes/ccspf_spec.md` §3's
pass 1 picture-frames "every framing edge, block side, **buck** and hanger flange" — the foam
is sprayed *onto* the buck. Two reasons, and the second is decisive:

1. The foam then bonds to **bare plywood**, which is exactly what its own evaluation report
   assumes. A membrane under it is an unevaluated bond line.
2. `[K6]`'s metal head flashing "starts on the foam face". A membrane applied before the
   foam would have the foam land over its outboard edge at the head — a **reverse lap**,
   driving water behind the membrane and into the reveal.

So the build order in `outie_window_truss_detail.md` stands: step 2 sets the bucks, step 8
sprays and shaves the foam, step 9 flashes.

**One continuous perimeter application, in one visit.** Sill, both jambs and the head are one
monolithic band — that is the entire reason the membrane is fluid-applied here, and splitting
it into a "sill pan" operation and a "jamb flashing" operation re-introduces the lapped
corner the liquid exists to remove. One trade, one arrival, one opening finished before the
next is started.

Sequence at an opening:

1. **Gauge the foam** at the reveal. It is shaved 1/2" behind the block's outer face; a
   ragged or under-shaved face is a substrate problem, not a coverage problem.
2. **Joint filler** at every inside corner and at the buck-to-foam junction — the
   fillet the membrane spans. Not optional; a liquid bridging a square cold joint cracks
   there first.
3. **Sill first, and heavy** — §1 item 4. **Slope first:** a tapered KDAT shim on the buck,
   or a preformed sloped pan, falling **1/4"/ft minimum** outward; a level pan is a ponding
   pan. A **rigid back-dam strip** (1/2" x 1-1/2" KDAT) at the sheathing face, so the dam
   stands on something. Back dam up the strip, across the sloped sill, out to the mount
   plane, and turn it **down over the buck's front edge**. The buck spans the 1/2" vent gap,
   so that is the outlet: water runs down past the sill course face and the cladding's back,
   hidden behind the sill trim (owner, 2026-10-16). No visible drip.
4. **Jambs and head, continuous into the sill's corners**, without stopping.
4a. **Head: bed the course, then strip in after the metal.** Membrane on the KDAT head
   course's top before `[K6]` goes on (§4). After it, a 4" strip of the same membrane over
   the upstand's top edge, 2" down the metal and 2" up the foam. Without it, water on the
   foam face runs **behind** the upstand: the head band stops at head + 2", and the upstand
   starts at head + 3 1/2".
4b. **Set the window on the pan, and leave its SILL FLANGE UNSEALED.** Seal the head and
   jamb flanges to the membrane; the sill flange stays open so the pan drains. Sealing all
   four, as many install sheets show, turns a draining pan into a ponding one. The interior
   air seal is low-expansion foam or backer rod and sealant between the frame and the buck,
   **inboard** of the back dam, all four sides.
5. **Lap onto the shaved ccSPF**, the extent the maker's written substrate approval names
   (§1 item 3). Until that letter exists, this step is unevaluated.
6. **Gauge the wet film** while it is wet. There is no second chance to measure it.

**Weather is a hold, not a preference** — the same register `ccspf_spec.md` §3 sets.

**Cold is a CURE problem, not an application problem, and that distinction is the trap.**
FastFlash may be applied to a frost-free substrate below 32 F, and the TDS says so, which
reads like permission. But it **will not begin to cure until temperatures rise above freezing
and stay there**. In a St. Paul January an applied film can sit uncured for weeks, and **you
cannot set a window onto an uncured membrane**. So:

- Apply only when the forecast holds above freezing through the maker's full cure window, or
- Accept that the opening stays open, uncured and unglazed until it does.

Neither is a schedule anybody wants in January. Flash the openings in the same weather window
the foam was sprayed in (60-80 F, `ccspf_spec.md` §3) and set the windows behind it.

## 4. What is NOT covered here

- **The metal head flashing (`[K6]`)** and the **sealant at the cladding-to-frame joint
  (`[K7]`)**. Different parts, different trades, bought elsewhere: the brake-formed pieces
  ride inside the panel's $/SF rate under TRIM IS PARITY, and the sealant is
  `[allowances] envelope-exterior-sealants-and-caulking`. What `[K6]` must be (owner,
  2026-10-16; `Project.opening_flashing_material`):
  - **Steel from the cladding maker's trim kit**, 24 ga PVDF in the house dark
    (`metal-dark-exterior`), never aluminium: it bears on the KDAT head course, and
    `notes/beam_water_protection.md` (AWC DCA6) forbids aluminium on treated wood.
  - **Separated from the KDAT** even so: it is bedded on this note's membrane or a butyl tape
    over the course top (§3 step 4a).
  - **End dams**: each end of the shedding leg folded up 3/4", and the piece runs 1" past
    each casing end, so water leaves at the drip, not off the ends behind the cladding.
  - The upstand's top edge is stripped in (§3 step 4a).
- **The two exterior swing doors.** Their pans are §6, billed on their own row.
- **The garage openings and the court openings.** §7 and §8.
- **Anything the engine grades.** No check in `haus check` reads a flashing membrane, its
  thickness, its substrate or its lap. The 3D and the 2D detail draw it from one set of
  numbers (`resolve/flashing_config.FLASHING`), and `integrity.opening_flashing_coverage`
  names an exterior opening that draws none (§8's two). A 0-FAIL report says nothing about
  this note.

The decks and service treads now match in Trex Enhance Basics, actual 0.94 inch stock;
the structural rough sill remains KDAT. No continuous board crosses the pan discharge.
See `composite_decking.md`.

**`D-G-SERVICE` is the exposed one.** Its threshold is 12" above the garage plate, so the
door stands on a framed rough sill (`W-G-S` `sill-0`, on one cripple). The top of that sill is
0'-0", flush with `FS-BW-FLOOR`'s boards, and the leaf swings out over the deck.

- **Sill and cripple are KDAT**, not the wall's SPF
  (`FramingSpec.exterior_door_sill_material_ref`).
- **Sloped pan, back dam, end dams.** Use a preformed sloped pan, or an impermeable
  self-adhered membrane (§1.1) over a beveled KDAT shim that falls outward. The back dam
  stands under the frame's interior edge. The end dams turn up the jambs at least 6", and the
  corners are the liquid.
- **The front leg turns DOWN past the cladding** into the gap between the board ends and the
  RO. That gap stays open: never caulk it, shim it, or run a board tight to the sill.
- Set the frame on sealant at the back dam only, never across the front, so the pan drains.
- The deck stays at 0'-0". A 1" drop (R311.3.1 allows 1 1/2" at an outswing door) would
  re-level the shared deck under `D-M-ENTRY` too; the pan and the frame's own sill height do
  that job instead.

`D-M-ENTRY` sits at its storey's floor and has no rough sill. Its pan is the same part,
lapped onto the house's fluid-applied system (§3). **Unlike a window's, a door pan runs past
the cladding** (the model draws it 1/2" past the cladding face): a threshold has no sill
trim to hide behind, and stopping it at the mount plane would discharge behind the panel.

## 7. Garage openings and the overhead door (2026-10-16)

The garage wall is `GARAGE_WALL_2X6`: 2" ccSPF in the bays, 5/8" CDX and the corrugated panel,
with **no WRB** and no exterior foam. So the CDX is the substrate and the plane the membrane
laps onto, and the opening is an innie: no buck projection, no vent gap.

- **Windows and `D-G-SERVICE`**: §3's sequence on CDX. The pan runs out past the panel to a
  drip, 1/4"/ft on a tapered KDAT shim, back dam on a strip at the studs' inner face. The head
  is §4's steel, over the casing with end dams, and its 3" upstand is stripped in to the CDX.
  CDX is on every qualifying report's substrate list, so §1 item 3's open item does not arise.
- **`D-G-OVERHEAD`**: head band and both jambs on the buck, lapped 2" onto the CDX. **No pan**:
  the opening runs to the slab. The jamb membrane **stops at the framed wall's base** (-1'-0",
  the ICF stem top), where the stem's Z flashing (`TR-G-STEMZ-*`, now with a 2" back leg up the
  CDX) takes over. Below it is the stem end, not this wall. Bought by
  `[allowances] envelope-garage-door-flashing`; the head metal is in the panel's trim.
- **Aluminium never touches the steel panel.** The stem Z and band are aluminium; the opening
  heads are steel. Where they meet, EPDM or sealant between.

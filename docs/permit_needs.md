# Permit Base Needs
## Standard Residential Building Permit Plan Packet
When submitting for a standard residential house building permit, the core of the packet consists of the construction drawings, often referred to as blueprints. While specific engineering requirements vary by local building codes and climate zones, a standard set of plans generally must include the following components:

### Site Plan
* Property Boundaries: Exact lot dimensions and property lines.
* Footprint: The exact location of the proposed house and any existing structures on the lot.
* Setbacks: Distances from the proposed structure to the property lines to prove zoning compliance.
* Grading and Drainage: Topography details showing how the land slopes and how rainwater will be directed away from the structure.
* Access and Utilities: Location of the driveway, easements, and connections for water, sewer, and gas.

### Floor Plans
* Room Layouts: A top-down view of every level of the home, including basements, garages, and decks.
* Dimensions: Exact room sizes, wall thicknesses, and overall square footage.
* Openings: Locations and sizes of all doors and windows, often accompanied by a dedicated schedule detailing the exact models.
* Fixed Elements: Placement of plumbing fixtures, cabinets, and major appliances.

### Exterior Elevations
* Directional Views: Flat, scale drawings of the front, rear, left, and right sides of the exterior.
* Heights: Overall building height from grade to the roof peak, as well as floor-to-floor heights.
* Materials: Callouts specifying exterior finishes, including siding type, roofing materials, and trim details.
* Roof Pitch: The angle and slope of the roof.

### Foundation Plan
* Foundation Type: Specific layout for a slab, crawlspace, or basement.
* Footings: Size, depth, and reinforcement $rebar$ details for the structural base spreading the load to the soil.
* Structural Supports: Locations of load-bearing walls, concrete piers, and steel columns.

### Framing Plans
* Floor Framing: Size, spacing, and direction of floor joists or engineered I-joists.
* Wall Framing: Details on stud sizes, spacing, and shear wall locations for wind or seismic bracing.
* Roof Framing: Layout of rafters or engineered roof trusses, including overhang dimensions and structural headers over large windows and doors.### Cross Sections and Structural Details
* Building Sections: Vertical "cut-through" views showing how the foundation, walls, floors, and roof integrate and connect.
* Wall Assembly: Detailed layers of the exterior walls, including framing, insulation type/thickness, vapor barriers, exterior sheathing, and drywall.
* Connection Details: Zoomed-in drawings showing how critical structural elements $like a deck ledger board attaching to the main house$ are fastened together.

### MEP (Mechanical, Electrical, and Plumbing) Plans
* Electrical Layout: Locations of all outlets, switches, light fixtures, smoke/carbon monoxide detectors, and the main electrical service panel.
* Plumbing Layout: General routing of incoming water supply lines and outgoing wastewater/venting pipes, plus the water heater location.
* Mechanical Layout: Location of the HVAC equipment, ductwork routing, and exhaust vents for bathrooms and kitchens.
